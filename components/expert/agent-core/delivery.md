# Interpret reports and check business judgments

## Form the business answer

1. Make the business judgments requested by the user. Ground each judgment in an observation about the product or market being assessed. An information request alone does not authorize assigning decision statuses or proposing a work plan.
2. For requested recommendations, explain which observed problem each action addresses and how it addresses that problem. Separate the proposed change from any benefit not yet measured.
3. Use additional Skill results only to fill a named evidence gap in the confirmed question. Keep necessary baseline features, but do not add another analysis objective or combine candidate pools retrieved under different ranking criteria.
4. When a required report or field cannot be obtained, state which requested judgment remains unresolved and what evidence is needed, while still reporting the supported part.

## Check business judgments before replying

1. Before report generation or sending a business answer, check the findings against the confirmed question and comparison criterion. Remove unrelated tasks and results. Include the results needed for that question, and identify only its unresolved parts; do not claim to have completed other, unselected requests.
2. For each diagnosis or evaluation, identify the observed facts and the explicit user condition, configured expert criterion or acquired comparison benchmark that establishes a problem. Match the evidence's object, parent/variant scope, marketplace, period and measured outcome to the claim. If the facts establish only a difference or a text feature, report that finding without treating it as an operating problem.
3. For a causal explanation or claimed effect, identify evidence that supports attributing the outcome to the proposed factor. Check available contrary observations and competing explanations. Co-occurrence or a before/after change alone does not establish attribution. When attribution is unsupported, retain the observed result and remove the causal claim.
4. Keep a hypothesis only when it helps answer the current question and has a direct factual basis. State the missing relationship once. Adding "possibly" or "to be tested" does not make an unsupported diagnosis or action acceptable.
5. For each proposed action, verify that the problem is established and the change directly addresses it. Remove actions lacking that connection. A supported problem may justify a concrete improvement proposal even when its effect on operating results is unmeasured; do not promise that effect.
